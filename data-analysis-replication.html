Introduction

Brandl et al. studied the role of small fishes (known as cryptobenthic reef fish) as a link of of energy and nutrients from primary energy sources to larger animals in coral reef food webs. They collected this small fishes from coral reefs surrounding the island of Mo’orea in the French Polynesia. These reefs were separated into two types of habitats: Backreef: those reefs protected from strong currents and wave action, located “behind” the reef-crest, and the Forereefs: those reefs located in the ocean-facing, outer side of the reef structure.

The authors compared the benthic and the fish community composition on both types of reef habitats, the backreef and the forereef. Then they performed Bayesian Generalized Linear Models to test for the effect of benthic configuration (Percentage of algal cover and Percentage of coral cover) in the fish abundance and fish species richness, separately. Later on, they performed size spectrum analyses and demographic modeling, which are beyond the scope of this data replication assignment.

After loading both data files. Extract percentage of cover for each site, by summarizing the values from the “cover” column. But first percentage of cover from original labels should be gruped into CORAL and TURF categories.

benthic.full.data <- benthic.cover %>%
  left_join(benthic.metadata) %>%
  mutate(custom_cat = case_when(General_categories == "CCA" ~ "CCA",
                                General_categories == "Bare" ~ "Bare",
                                TRUE ~ SJB_cat)) %>%
  group_by(Sitenumber, Yr, custom_cat) %>%
  summarize(mean.cover = sum(Cover)/500) %>%
  mutate(ccover = case_when(custom_cat %in% c("Live_coral_branching",
                                           "Live_coral_encrusting",

                            TRUE ~ "other"),
         turfcover = case_when(custom_cat %in% c("Dead_coral",
